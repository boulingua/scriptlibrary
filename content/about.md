---
title: How this reading room works
---

Read a book you can already understand, with a small share of its letters drawn from another alphabet. The words stay in the original language. The aim is to become comfortable seeing unfamiliar letter forms during sustained reading.

Start with **20% of eligible letters**, then increase or decrease the slider. Choose **Original text** at any time. Modern Greek, Russian Cyrillic and Ukrainian have separate mappings. The Nordic option introduces only a few suitable Norwegian and Swedish letters.

The sound matches are approximate and depend on the source language. For example, Modern Greek **β** represents a v-like sound, while Ukrainian **ґ** corresponds to hard g. German **ö** can be practised as Norwegian **ø**. German ö is **not** changed to Russian ё, which has a different sound.

Some letters and contexts are deliberately skipped. The percentage is a sample of eligible letters, so 20% does not mean one fifth of all characters will change. Every PDF gives its actual replacement counts. An unchanged copy of every book is available.

The online reader remembers only your chosen alphabet and density in this browser. Text and fonts are hosted with the library. No account, analytics or third-party font service is used. Screen readers and pronunciation tools should use the original text: mixed scripts can change how they speak.

The downloads contain complete original-language narratives, not excerpts. *Candide* omits the later editorial preface and footnotes; the Alice edition omits illustrations. Historical spelling is retained. These are reading exercises, not new translations, phonetic transcriptions, or claims of curriculum conformance.

The same versioned mapping engine powers the [Chrome extension](../extension/), the online reader and the generated PDFs. The supplied Azbuka prototype contributed the ideas of staged introduction and an immediate original-text view.
