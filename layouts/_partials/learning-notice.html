For learning purposes only

This is a deliberately simplified exercise for becoming familiar with letter forms. It leaves out important aspects of pronunciation, spelling, stress, rhythm, dialect and language use. It is not a translation, a complete phonetic transcription or a substitute for learning the language. “100%” means all eligible letters, not every letter in the text.

Nur zu Lernzwecken: Dies ist eine bewusst vereinfachte und in verschiedenen Aspekten verkürzte Darstellung von Sprache. Aussprache, Rechtschreibung, Betonung, regionale Varianten und sprachliche Zusammenhänge werden nur teilweise berücksichtigt. Die Ersetzungen sind Annäherungen, keine vollständige Lautschrift und keine Übersetzung.

Cyrillic: countries, languages and differences

Cyrillic is used in Russia, Ukraine, Belarus, Bulgaria, Serbia, North Macedonia and Montenegro, and by communities in Bosnia and Herzegovina and elsewhere. Rusyn has regional Cyrillic standards. Many minority languages and languages outside Europe also use Cyrillic.

There is no single Cyrillic alphabet for all these languages. Russian г usually represents /g/; Ukrainian г represents /ɦ/ and ґ represents /g/. Ukrainian and Belarusian use і; Belarusian also uses ў. Serbian and Macedonian use ј, љ and њ and have other distinct letters. Bulgarian ъ is a vowel; Russian ъ is a hard sign. Our one Cyrillic option uses a limited shared subset and omits uncertain pairs.

Nordic letters: Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Iceland

“Nordic” is a learning label, not one alphabet or pronunciation system. Danish and Norwegian use æ, ø and å. Swedish uses å, ä and ö; Finnish also uses ä and ö, while å mainly occurs in Swedish names. Icelandic uses æ, ö, þ, ð and accented vowels; Faroese has its own conventions, including ð, æ and ø.

Similar-looking letters need not have the same sound. Icelandic æ usually represents /ai/, unlike the æ-like vowel practised here in selected English words. Icelandic þ and ð resemble the two English “th” sounds, but cannot replace a single English letter. Danish and Norwegian vowel sounds also vary with context and dialect. This set uses only a few approximate Danish/Norwegian and Swedish matches; it does not teach every Nordic letter.
